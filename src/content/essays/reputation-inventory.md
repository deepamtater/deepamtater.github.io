An a16z partner just said young founders aren't afraid to fail. He described a real pattern. The reason behind it is simpler than bravery.

Troy Kirwin invests at pre-seed through a16z speedrun. He observed that young founders will launch, fail, and relaunch something completely different a month later. They cold-email founders they admire and actually get replies. Senior founders won't because they fear the signal.

Troy framed it as courage. I've lived it and I think it's simpler than that.

Founders accumulate what I'd call reputation inventory. Every LinkedIn connection, every "excited to announce" post adds to it. A founder who launched publicly to thousands of connections six months ago can't quietly pivot. Pivoting means a public retraction in front of everyone who commented "congrats, this is amazing" four months ago.

Young founders pay almost nothing to fail because the audience watching was tiny.

I relaunched my entire product 7 months in. Different positioning, different site. About 240 users on the old version and a retention problem I couldn't fix without rebuilding. Nobody in my network noticed. Not one person asked what happened.

I've cold-emailed founders I had no business contacting. Most replied. I think they could tell I had nothing to sell and no image to protect.

The advantage compounds in reverse. Every connection you earn makes the next pivot more expensive. Mine was free because nobody was watching.
